## 1. SMART (criteria for writing a goal or KPI)
- Origin: George T. Doran, *Management Review*, Nov 1981, vol 70 no 11, p.35. https://www.decisionskills.com/uploads/5/1/6/0/5160560/doran_1981_s.m.a.r.t-way-management-review.pdf
- Principles, in Doran's original wording: "Specific—target a specific area for improvement. Measurable—quantify or at least suggest an indicator of progress. Assignable—specify who will do it. Realistic—state what results can realistically be achieved, given available resources. Time-related—specify when the result(s) can be achieved."
- Doran says not every objective needs all five: "the suggested acronym doesn't mean that every objective written will have all five criteria."
- The common modern wording is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Doran's original differs ("Assignable", "Realistic"). https://www.jfarrington.com/2014/01/objectify-this-a-brief-history-of-smart-criteria/ and https://www.projectsmart.co.uk/smart-goals/brief-history-of-smart-goals.php ("SMART does not have one definitive meaning").
- Use case: a quality check on an individual KPI or objective, at any level. It does not choose which KPIs to have.

## 2. Balanced Scorecard (BSC)
- Origin: Kaplan & Norton, "The Balanced Scorecard—Measures that Drive Performance", HBR, Jan–Feb 1992. https://hbr.org/1992/01/the-balanced-scorecard-measures-that-drive-performance-2
- Principles: four perspectives, so that financial results are not the only measures. HBS Online: "combines the traditional financial perspective with additional perspectives that focus on customers, internal business processes, and learning and development." Perspectives are Financial, Customer, Internal business process, and Learning & growth. https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/balanced-scorecard
- Method: the scorecard is built from strategy through a strategy map. In Kaplan and Norton's workshop process, executives define key success factors and then propose measures for each perspective. They narrow the measures afterwards. (HBS Online; "Putting the Balanced Scorecard to Work" excerpt at simonfoucher.com)
- Use case: translating organisation-wide strategy into a balanced KPI set across several functions. It is heavier to set up than the others.

## 3. OKR (Objectives and Key Results)
- Origin and promotion: created by Andy Grove at Intel and spread by John Doerr (*Measure What Matters*). https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/okrs-objectives-key-results-explanation-examples
- Principles: an Objective is "significant, concrete, action-oriented, and inspirational". A Key Result is "specific and time-bound, aggressive yet realistic, and measurable and verifiable". The same page advises no more than five Key Results per Objective, and fewer than three Objectives. Cycles are usually quarterly.
- Google's OKRs are not used for bonuses or promotions, according to Doerr (https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/why-okrs-john-joerr). This is vendor/advocate material.
- Use case: aligning teams on a few ambitious goals each cycle, with transparency.
- My inference, unsourced: OKRs are goal-steering, not a standing KPI set. Teams often run KPIs for ongoing health and OKRs for change.

## 4. Goal–Question–Metric (GQM)
- Origin: Victor Basili's work with NASA. Slide citation: Basili, Caldiera, Rombach 1994, "The Goal Question Metric Approach". https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2009/CMMI/9146TuesdayTrack4Campo.pdf
- Principles: "for an organization to measure in a purposeful way it must first specify the goals… then it must trace those goals to the data". The model has three levels: goal, question, metric. A goal specifies purpose, object, issue and viewpoint. Each goal is refined into questions, and each question into metrics. http://cs.umd.edu/~mvz/handouts/gqm.pdf
- Example from the paper: goal "improve the timeliness of change request processing" from the project manager's viewpoint. Metrics: average cycle time, standard deviation, % cases outside the upper limit.
- Use case: deriving metrics (software and process measurement) so that no KPI lacks a goal.
